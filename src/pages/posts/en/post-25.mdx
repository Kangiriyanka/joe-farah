## Intro

&nbsp;


For any average Spanish learner, verb conjugations are particularily difficult to wrap your head around. 
In my experience, a mix of table drilling, reading the <a class="secondary-a "href= "https://www.bbc.com/mundo">news</a>, and speaking with native speakers produces satisfactory results.<sup><a class="secondary-a" href="#footnotes">1.</a></sup>
Verbs are all the more better practiced when applying them to your personal world. If you're into snowboarding, learning the conjugation of verbs like slide, jump and fall on your behind is ideal for describing your exhilarating glades.

&nbsp;

Another decent strategy is actively listening to your favourite episode of a TV show on repeat with Spanish subtitles and/or audio, while getting a feel for how they're applied — my go-to media is  <a class="secondary-a "href= "https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slam_Dunk_(manga)">Slam Dunk</a>. 







&nbsp;



## Joe's Conjugator

&nbsp;

If I may, I recommend downloading the Spanish verb conjugation app called <a className="secondary-a" href="https://apps.apple.com/ca/app/conjugato-learn-spanish-verbs/id1388189747"> ConjuGato</a> to refresh your memory on all the available patterns, since the app does a terrific job at supplying
comprehensive tables with explanations. 

&nbsp;

Out of curiosity for its inner workings, I put my own spin on coding a basic conjugator as an exercise. In sum, working around the irregular verbs was simultaneously  stimulating and frustrating, because the 
exceptions can be found in the stem, the ending or both. 

&nbsp;


For example,  the verb <u>poder</u> is irregular in the indicative preterite form, as every "o" in its stem gets replaced with "u". Moreover, the first and third singular person have irregular endings where í changes to e and ío changes to ó respectively. 


&nbsp;

In the Joe conjugator <sup><a class="secondary-a" href="#footnotes">2.</a></sup>, the irregular changes on the stem are highlighted in <span className="text-[#308f4d]"> green</span> for substituted/added letters and <span className="underline decoration-[#308f4d]"> underlined</span> for removed letters. Irregular endings are highlighted in <span className="text-[#ea6565]">red</span>. Wild verb conjugations defying human logic, in highly irregular verbs such as ser and ir, are marked in <span className="text-[#3384ed]"> blue</span>.
For comparison purposes, tapping the <u>Show Unmodified</u> button allows you to see the  <span className=" text-[#D878F0]"> conjugation </span> had we ignored the rules and conjugated with the standard rules (not explained in this post). <sup><a class="secondary-a" href="#footnotes">3.</a></sup>
